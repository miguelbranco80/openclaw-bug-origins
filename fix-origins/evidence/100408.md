## Fix PR #172933 description (by steipete, opened 2026-10-15T08:10:27Z)

```text
## What Problem This Solves

A Gateway restart or operator cancellation could push an automation over its consecutive-failure threshold and disable it, even though the interruption was not a job failure.

## User Impact

Recurring automations keep their existing failure streak and remain enabled after an interruption. Interruption history remains visible. Interrupted one-shots remain disabled to avoid replaying work whose side effects are uncertain. Existing disabled automations are not automatically re-enabled.

## Why This Change Was Made

Startup recovery counted abandoned executions as failures and could queue auto-disable or repair notifications. Normal finalization also counted explicitly aborted runs. Remove that startup failure machinery and use the existing cancellation classification to exclude aborted outcomes from failure streaks, auto-disable, and failure notifications. Genuine timeouts and job failures keep the existing policy. Error scheduling also stays unchanged: cancellation completion anchors backoff using the preserved count (including the existing minimum floor at zero). This keeps finalization and maintenance consistent without adding stored classification fields. No schema, stored-format, or configuration changes.
```
