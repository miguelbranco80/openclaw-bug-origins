## What Problem This Solves

Fixes misleading update failure reports when Doctor lint reaches its deadline or automatic repair cannot start an inference turn.

Related: #152759, #151546, #153049. Thanks to @Lendersmark for the detailed Linux timing analysis and elimination of competing causes.

## User Impact

Future updaters distinguish a complete lint report followed by a process/pipe stall from checks that have not produced a report. Unavailable automatic repair is recorded as skipped, preserving the original failed check as the cause. Real lint findings and failed validation remain authoritative.

This is a diagnostic fix. It cannot patch the installed 2026.9.4 updater, and it does not claim to fix an identified leaked handle on the reporter's host.

## Why This Change Was Made

The published 2026.9.4 driver shares one 300-second budget across snapshot preparation and all candidate checks. Its final elapsed-time suffix measures the entire rehearsal, while each step has its own duration. The cited 299-second total and 22–40-second lint duration are consistent with lint starting about 259–277 seconds into that window. The driver also copies the aggregate log tail into the failed step: `Doctor complete.` belongs to ordinary repair and is not lint's terminal JSON. Those artifact fields alone therefore do not establish that lint completed before termination. Published 2026.9.5 already invokes the shared one-shot exit helper after lint.

The canary owner now freezes same-child report and process-exit evidence before teardown, keeps explicit timeout metadata, and distinguishes open output pipes from a process that has not exited. It reports a kill only when signal and cleanup evidence support it. Existing lint findings retain their full capped budget; process context uses the existing diagnostic tail. The repair owner records inference unavailability before any turn as skipped. This complements #153147, whose scope excludes repair-path implementation.
