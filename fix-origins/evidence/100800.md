## Fix PR #171370 description (by RaphazZze, opened 2026-10-13T21:06:16Z)

```text
Closes #171369

## What Problem This Solves

Fixes: Active Memory skips deep recall in its default escalate mode when users ask about earlier conversations in French.

## User Impact

French questions such as "Tu te souviens de ce qu'on a décidé pour le serveur ?" or "Qu'est-ce qu'on a dit pour Kyoto ?" now trigger recall when the lane-1 lookup has no strong hit. Unaccented spellings and curly apostrophes (common from mobile keyboards) are supported. Future reminders (with a future cue such as *demain* or *ce soir*) and everyday statements stay quiet; an ambiguous "Tu te souviens de/d' + X ?" goes to the recall model. Other languages and configuration defaults are unchanged.

## Why This Change Was Made

Adds a French entry to the existing localized `{ intent, future, retrospective }` structure. Intent requires either recall-question phrasing (*tu te souviens*, *rappelle-moi ce que…*) or completed-discussion context (*on en a parlé*, *je t'ai dit*, *qu'est-ce qu'on a décidé*, a past-time marker near a discussion verb). Bare passé composé such as "On a décidé d'aller au cinéma ce soir." does not match: French *on* usually narrates the user's own life, not the conversation with the assistant. *Rappelle-moi de…* (remind me to) is a reminder and stays quiet.

## Evidence

**Review findings addressed** (`2117351`, supersedes the infinitive rule from `6c7877f`):
- The `-er`/`-ir` lookahead after "tu te souviens de/d'" is removed: it rejected proper names ("Tu te souviens de Roger ?") as remember-to verbs, and spelling can't separate the two. "Tu te souviens d'envoyer le rapport ?" (with or without "aujourd'hui") and "Vous vous rappelez de faire les courses ?" now **start recall**; the same questions with a future cue ("… demain ?") stay quiet through the French `future` guard.
- New positives: "Tu te souviens de Roger ?", "Te souviens-tu de Xavier ?", "Vous vous rappelez de Gauthier ?", "Tu te souviens d'Olivier ?", "Tu te souviens d'envoyer le rapport ?".

**Earlier review findings** (`6c7877f`):
- Discussion verbs and time markers carry letter/number boundaries in every alternative, so "crédit", "audit" and "cahier" no longer match `dit` / `hier`.
```
