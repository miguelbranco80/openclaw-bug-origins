## What Problem This Solves

Updates and repairs could reject a stopped systemd unit as an ownership change and leave the Gateway down; standalone Doctor could finish repairing an already-stopped Gateway without starting it.

## User Impact

Managed Gateways can recover after failed updates and repairs, including restarting the previous installation after rollback. Git-to-npm updates refresh the service to use the new installation. Successful standalone `doctor --fix` also starts and verifies an already-stopped, owned Gateway targeting the current installation.

Ownership, current-authority, installation-drift, and data-safety checks remain enforced. Update-time Doctor leaves activation with its updater.

## Why This Change Was Made

systemd can collect an inactive unit while its installed definition remains intact. Requiring an already-loaded command then failed before the updater could refresh the launcher or recover the previous service. The update service-inspection owner now uses its retained manager identity and current native/caller authority to reload unit metadata. Restart preparation, failure recovery, and rollback share that operation. Doctor reuses its existing restoration and readiness owner for a successfully repaired, previously stopped service.

The three expensive legacy-driver cases now use the existing opt-in live tier, collected by `vitest.live.config.ts` and `pnpm test:all`. Their shared assertions are preserved; the remaining 14 cases run in ordinary PR CI. No new test tier or configuration was introduced.

Thanks @Matuno, @DonnieFi, and @Navras98 for the reports.
